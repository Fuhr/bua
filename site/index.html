The whole idea

Nightfall.

At the start of a session the lotus is a full open bloom in sage. As you work, the petals gather into a bud and the colour travels through the day — jade, lotus pink, sunset coral — until, at the limit, it rests as a closed bud in twilight purple. Then the session resets, and it blooms again.

What it is

A small, kind thing.

Not a dashboard. Just a quiet companion that turns a number you'd rather not watch into something worth glancing at.

  • Breathesa slow 7-second breath, glowing softly behind the bloom
  • Showshow much of your session & weekly limit remains, and when each resets
  • Greets youwith a quote each time you open the panel
  • Appearsanywhere with ⌃⌥B — no cursor needed
  • Restsif it can't reach the garden — calm, never alarmed

Privacy

It never reads your work.

Bua reads your Claude Code token read-only and sends it to exactly one place — the same usage endpoint Claude Code's own /usage screen uses.

It never sees your conversations, and never stores or refreshes anything. If anything fails, the lotus simply rests.

Get it

Two minutes to bloom.

Bua is a personal app, shared by a friend — so it isn't signed by Apple. macOS will warn you once; here's the way past it.

Apple Silicon Mac macOS 15 (Sequoia)+ Claude Code, logged in
  1. Drag Bua.app into your Applications folder.
  2. Double-click it. macOS says it "can't verify Bua is free of malware" — that's expected. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click Open Anyway. Confirm with Touch ID.
  4. Look for the little lotus in your menu bar. You only do this once. 🪷
Comfortable with Terminal? Skip the warning entirely — paste this:
xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Bua.app

No Claude Code yet? Bua will still install — the lotus just rests, calmly, until there's a session to watch.
